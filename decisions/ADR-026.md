# ADR-026 — Distributed console architecture: one Brain, stateless consoles, a private tailnet
**2026-07-11 · Accepted** — records the operator's 2026-07-11 directive ("JarvisOS is a distributed personal AI system") in the only form that preserves the frozen architecture: consoles are L5 interfaces.

**Context.** The operator will spend ~95% of their time on a Samsung Galaxy Tab S7+ and will approve/monitor from an Android phone; the Windows PC (Ryzen 7 7700X, RTX 4070 Ti, 31 GB RAM) hosts everything that computes. ARCHITECTURE.md already defines L5 INTERFACES as thin clients that "render, they do not own state." The distributed change therefore requires no redesign — but without an explicit decision, the gravitational pull of convenience (caching chats on the tablet, storing an API key in a phone app, syncing memory for offline reading) would erode Law 5 one small convenience at a time.

**Decision.**

1. **Roles.** The PC is the **Brain**: sole host of the kernel, models, MCP servers, gateway seam, memory, config, secrets, and all execution. The Tab S7+ is the **Primary Console**; the phone is the **Secondary Console** (notifications, approvals, quick commands). Consoles are L5 clients, nothing more.
2. **Stateless consoles.** No canonical state on any console, ever: no memory files, no config, no journals, no secrets, no API keys. A console may hold ephemeral render state (an open chat view); losing or wiping a console must cost **zero information** (Law 5 applied to devices).
3. **Tailnet communication.** All console↔Brain traffic crosses a private WireGuard-based tailnet (Tailscale; named successor: plain WireGuard — Law 4). **No public exposure:** no port forwarding, no reverse proxy on the open internet, no Brain service bound beyond localhost + tailnet interface.
4. **Authentication.** Device identity = tailnet membership under the operator's single tailnet account. A device not on the tailnet cannot reach any Brain service. Input arriving over the authenticated tailnet from an enrolled console is **T0 operator instruction** (ADR-013); the same words arriving any other way are not. Lost/stolen console = remove the device from the tailnet (one click) — nothing to wipe because of item 2.
5. **Remote UI.** All console UIs are served *from the Brain* over the tailnet (web UIs or kernel-native remote surfaces). Consoles bring a browser/app shell; versions, features, and state live Brain-side. Uploads from consoles (camera, files) land in a Brain-side intake directory and are classified per ADR-013 before anything trusts them (an operator's *photo* is T0 intent; a photographed *document's contents* are T2/T3 data).
6. **Outbox execution.** The Outbox (ADR-012) renders and approves on any console; it **executes only on the Brain**, in the ACTOR context (ADR-009/020). An approval tap is a T0 signal; the action itself never runs console-side.
7. **Security boundaries.** The Brain's firewall posture, the egress allowlist (ADR-013), and the ACTOR sandbox (ADR-020) are unchanged by this ADR — consoles add *no* new inbound trust beyond the tailnet's device auth. Console OS hygiene (screen lock, OS updates) is recorded in OPERATIONS.md as operator responsibility.

**Consequences.** The operator gets the tablet-first experience with zero new state-management burden: every console is disposable by construction. Cost: the Brain must be reachable (always-on when needed — its stated role), and a tailnet outage degrades to "walk to the PC," never to data loss. Phase 0 cost is three app installs; everything else is later phases serving UIs over the same pipe.

**Alternatives considered.** (1) *Native console apps with local caches* — rejected: every cache is a Law 5 violation waiting for a lost tablet; revisit only per-surface, by ADR, with encrypted-and-revocable as the bar. (2) *Public HTTPS endpoint + login* — rejected: an internet-facing attack surface plus certificate/identity maintenance for a one-operator system, when a tailnet gives mutual auth for free (Law 10). (3) *Cloud relay (vendor remote-access services)* — rejected: routes the operator's traffic through a third party and violates local-first for zero functional gain over the tailnet. (4) *Syncthing/OneDrive for "offline memory" on the tablet* — rejected explicitly: this is the exact erosion path item 2 exists to block.

**Future review criteria.** Reopen if: (a) Tailscale's free tier or client health changes materially (quarterly pulse; successor is plain WireGuard, swap playbook per MAINTENANCE §2); (b) a genuine offline-console need emerges — that is a per-surface ADR with encryption and remote-wipe requirements, not a quiet cache; (c) a second *stateful* machine joins (a second Brain) — that is ADR-011/025's multi-machine trigger, a bigger decision than this ADR; (d) any Brain service is found bound beyond localhost/tailnet (watchdog check) — incident, not review.
