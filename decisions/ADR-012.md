# ADR-012 — The Outbox: all outward and destructive actions flow through draft → approve → execute
**2026-07-10 · Accepted** — ratifies [ARCHITECTURE-V2](../ARCHITECTURE-V2.md) §6/§12; makes Law 7 mechanical.

**Context.** The red-team review ([C5](../reviews/2026-07-09-red-team-review.md)) exposed the constitution and roadmap as contradictory from Phase 6 onward: Law 7 demands explicit approval for every destructive/outward action, while the roadmap runs the kernel headless from Task Scheduler at 8 a.m. There was no approval queue, no notification channel, no place a non-programmer could see "3 drafts pending." Autonomy versus oversight was never resolved — it was written down twice, incompatibly.

**Decision.** The Outbox is a first-class L3.5 component:

- Headless jobs (READER contexts, scheduled) may **draft** anything — emails, posts, commits, deletions — as entries in `outbox\pending\`, each carrying a plain-English summary, the exact action, and its blast radius.
- **Nothing in `pending\` executes.** Approval (via the Outbox UI or the morning brief reading the queue aloud) moves an entry to `outbox\approved\`; the ACTOR processor (ADR-009) executes only approved entries and logs to `outbox\done\`.
- No outward-facing capability (Phase 6) ships before the Outbox exists.
- Gating is proportional to consequence: irreversible, outward, or costly actions always gate; reversible local file operations inside the system's own tree do not. Approvals per day is a tracked metric in the weekly status; sustained >10/day is treated as a design incident (approval fatigue), answered with better batching and summaries — never with blanket pre-approval.

**Consequences.** Law 7 becomes structural: autonomy drafts, humans approve, and the two cannot be confused. The 8 a.m. brief becomes *"three replies are drafted and waiting; approve to send."* Cost: latency on outward actions and a real risk of approval fatigue — mitigated by batching and blast-radius-proportional gating, both unproven until real use (V2 §13 residual risk, tracked by the metric above).

**Alternatives considered.** (1) *Per-action interactive confirmation only* — rejected: does not exist in headless runs, which is exactly where C5 bites. (2) *Trusted-autonomy allowlists ("the agent may always send email to X")* — rejected for the baseline: standing permissions are how injection cashes out (ADR-009/013); any future allowlist requires its own superseding ADR. (3) *No headless autonomy at all* — rejected: forfeits the system's endgame (Phase 7) when a mechanism can reconcile the two. Note: MCP Apps (2026-07-28 spec) may let the Outbox UI ship as a single portable MCP server — an implementation candidate, evaluated in Phase 2, not a commitment of this ADR.

**Future review criteria.** Reopen if: (a) approvals/day sustainedly exceed 10 despite batching — the gating taxonomy needs redesign; (b) an approved-entry execution ever diverges from its approved summary — incident, and the proposal format gains whatever fidelity it lacked; (c) a kernel/OS-native approval mechanism matures to where it can *fill* this role's contract (queue durability, plain-English summaries, audit trail) — swap per MAINTENANCE.md playbook.
